Rosette Lab

Rosette Lab is a drawing app for making spirograph‑style art, turning photos into spirals, drawing with sound, and solving spiral puzzles. Here is how to get help with it.

The short version

  • Email chris@blackbarnlabs.com and a person reads it.
  • Tell us your iPhone or iPad model and your iOS version if you can — it helps a lot.
  • There are no accounts, no ads and no subscriptions. Nothing to cancel.

A good bug report tells us what you did, what you expected, and what happened instead. A screenshot or a decoder code that shows the problem is even better. We may not reply the same day, but every message is read.

02 · The five tabs

Create is where you draw. Pick gears, set the pen position and rotation, and the spiral appears as you go. Tap Share to send it out as a 4K PNG or an SVG. The info button opens About, and undo and redo sit in the top bar beside it.

Puzzles holds the Rosette Bureau case files. Each one hides a decoder code; solve the puzzle and the code unlocks something.

Photo turns one image of yours into spiral line art.

Sound has nine modes. Four are on the main picker: Listen, Note, Theremin and X/Y. The other five are under More modes: Find the Note, NamePlate, Duet, Plate shapes and Hum to Unlock.

Listen is the audio-driven screen: it hears what is going on around you and turns it into a running pattern of hats, solo, rhythm and kick with a live waveform. There is a Stop control on the screen.

Four places use the microphone in total: the Listen tab, and in the Sound tab, Listen, NamePlate and Hum to Unlock. The other six Sound modes make sound rather than listening for it.

03 · Decoder codes

Every drawing has a code. It's a short string that describes the gear settings exactly, shown in the Share sheet and in the drawing's detail view.

  • To save a design: keep the code. Entering it again later regenerates the drawing exactly.
  • To share a design: send the code instead of a picture. The other person types it in and gets the real thing, not a screenshot.
  • If a code won't open: check for a missing character at the start or end, and make sure there are no spaces added by your keyboard. Codes are case‑sensitive.

Codes only work in Rosette Lab. They are not web links and nothing is fetched when you enter one.

04 · Exporting

4K PNG is the best choice for printing or for sharing as a picture. It's saved at 3840 × 3840 pixels.

SVG is a vector file, so it stays sharp at any size — best if you plan to cut, engrave, print large, or edit the lines in another app.

GIF animates the spiral as it draws. If a GIF shows up as a still image somewhere, that app probably previews only the first frame; open it in Photos or a browser to see it move.

If the Share sheet shows an empty or black preview, the file itself is still fine. Tap Save Image and check it in Photos.

05 · Your gallery

Saved designs live in the Gallery, on your device, and can be renamed — try names like Aurora Bloom or Prairie Ember. Tap one to reopen it with its settings intact.

Your gallery is stored inside the app. There is no cloud copy and no account, so if you delete the app, the gallery goes with it. Export anything you want to keep. If iCloud Backup is on for your device, iOS may include app data in your own backup.

06 · Microphone, Listen and the Sound tab

Rosette Lab only asks for the microphone the first time you tap something that needs it. It is used in four places: the Listen tab, and in the Sound tab the Listen, NamePlate and Hum to Unlock modes. The other six Sound modes make sound rather than listening for it.

Audio is captured into memory, analyzed on your device, and thrown away. It is never written to a file and never sent anywhere, which is why listening works with no connection at all. The privacy policy has the detail.

If you tapped Don't Allow, or a screen stays silent, turn the microphone back on in iOS Settings › Rosette Lab › Microphone, or in Settings › Privacy & Security › Microphone. Everything else in the app keeps working without it.

In the Sound tab, the sand pattern sits still when the room is quiet. Hum, whistle or play a note and it moves.

07 · Photos

The Photo tab asks for access to the one picture you choose. Rosette Lab cannot browse your library; it only sees the image you hand it. Conversion happens on your device, so nothing is uploaded.

Lines come out best from photos with strong contrast — a silhouetted tree, a face in clear light, a building against sky. Busy backgrounds give busy spirals.

08 · Puzzles and stars

Each case file has a solution and earns stars. Progress is saved on your device as you go. There is no hint system and no hints to buy; the puzzles are meant to be sat with.

09 · Price, purchases and restoring

Rosette Lab is a one-time purchase, and there are no subscriptions and no ads.

Code Maker is a separate, optional $1.99 one-time unlock for writing your own message codes. It is sold on its own, so buying it does not change what the app costs. Open it from the Create tab and buy it there through the App Store. If you reinstall the app or move to a new iPhone or iPad, tap Restore Purchases on that screen and it comes back at no charge, as long as you are signed in to the same Apple account you bought it with. If it does not come back, check Settings › your name › Media & Purchases for the right account, then try again.

Refunds are handled by Apple, not by us, because Apple takes the payment. Request one at reportaproblem.apple.com.

10 · Requirements

Rosette Lab needs an iPhone or iPad running iOS or iPadOS 18.0 or later. It works fully offline, apart from buying or restoring Code Maker, which needs a connection to the App Store. There is no Mac version.

11 · Privacy

We collect nothing. No account, no analytics, no ads, no tracking, no third‑party code. The full detail, including what happens with the microphone and your photos, is in the privacy policy.
